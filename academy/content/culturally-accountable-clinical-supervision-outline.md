# Draft course 01: Culturally Accountable Clinical Supervision

Status: INTERNAL CONTENT DRAFT. No approval, CE hours, course duration, price, prerequisite or credit eligibility has been established. Do not advertise as board-approved or as clinical-supervisor qualification training.

**Proposed audience:** practicing clinical supervisors, supervisor candidates and mental-health leaders, subject to course-level prerequisites and future eligibility decisions.

**Learner outcome candidates:**

1. Distinguish documented supervisee performance and client-care concerns from untested assumptions about identity, communication and professionalism.
2. Identify an equitable method for distributing clinical caseload complexity, acuity, administrative tasks and support.
3. Construct a basic supervision-accountability process using transparent criteria, supervisee feedback, corrective action and review dates.

## Module A: Recognizing bias in supervision

- Recognize the difference between observable behavior, clinical competence and interpretive labels such as “professional,” “resistant” or “not a team player.”
- Case discussion: two supervisees receive different feedback after comparable interruptions during case presentation. Identify alternative explanations and evidence that should be gathered before drawing a conclusion.
- Draft exercise: rewrite impression-based feedback into an observable description with a concrete support plan.
- Required check for course design: does the lesson invite application to multiple identity and power contexts without assuming one shared experience?

## Module B: Equitable caseload assignment

- Map case complexity, required training and support, language access, documentation burden, trainee experience and supervision capacity.
- Case discussion: high-acuity cases cluster with one supervisee while another receives lower-acuity assignments. Develop a transparent case-allocation review and record justifications for clinical exceptions.
- Draft worksheet: case allocation criteria, exception reason, supervisor decision and scheduled re-evaluation.
- Flag that “equity” is not identical caseload counts and should not override clinical safety or scope-of-practice limits.

## Module C: Supervisor accountability and repair

- Establish a structured feedback process: issue, evidence, supervisee perspective, action, responsible person and follow-up date.
- Explore ways to review trends without exposing client-identifying data or treating a single score as definitive evidence of bias.
- Case discussion: a supervisee reports biased language in a performance assessment. Draft an impartial review and a no-retaliation response.
- Draft takeaway: a repeatable supervisory self-audit that records both process measures and corrective actions.
